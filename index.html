Simulation study

Can AI reduce OB claims before they happen?

We built a simulation of 10,000 pregnant patients to find out. Using open-source clinical data and evidence-based intervention models, we measured how much an AI system that flags care gaps in real time — before a bad outcome occurs — could reduce insurance claims, costs, and maternal harm.

How the simulation works

1

Generate a synthetic patient population

We used Synthea — an open-source, MIT-licensed patient generator — to create 10,000 synthetic pregnant patients with realistic demographics, conditions, and care histories. Demographic distributions were calibrated to CDC National Vital Statistics data.

2

Assign clinical conditions and baseline care gaps

Each patient was assigned conditions (gestational diabetes, preeclampsia, anemia, GBS, tobacco use) at rates matching HCUP NIS 2021 national benchmarks. Baseline guideline adherence was set at 60% — the documented real-world average — meaning 40% of patients had at least one preventable care gap.

3

Run the baseline scenario (no AI)

We simulated outcomes — claims, NICU admissions, readmissions, maternal deaths, severe morbidity — based purely on existing care quality. This is the counterfactual: what happens if nothing changes.

4

Apply 10 AI clinical decision support rules

We then ran the same population through an AI intervention model. Each rule fires when a patient is eligible and the AI system reaches them (75% average coverage). The physician then acts on the flag based on an evidence-calibrated response rate of 69%. Impact parameters come from Cochrane reviews, USPSTF, ACOG, and CDC guidelines.

5

Compare outcomes and run Monte Carlo validation

We measured the delta between scenarios across 8 clinical metrics, then ran 50 Monte Carlo iterations with randomized physician response rates to produce 90% confidence intervals. Even the pessimistic P5 outcome shows meaningful harm prevented.

Data sources

Synthea — primary simulation database

Open-source synthetic patient generator (MIT/MITRE). Free, privacy-safe, no data use agreement required. Pregnancy module generates realistic OB patient populations coded in SNOMED, LOINC, and RxNorm.

HCUP NIS 2021 — benchmark calibration

Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project, National Inpatient Sample. Used to calibrate baseline claim rates (8.2%), NICU admissions (7.2%), C-section rates (32.6%), and 30-day readmissions (4.8%).

CDC NVSS / WONDER 2021 — mortality & disparity rates

Source for maternal mortality (23.8 per 100,000), racial disparity multipliers (2.3× for Black mothers), postpartum depression rates (11.5%), and preterm birth rates (10.4%).

USPSTF / ACOG / WHO / Cochrane — intervention evidence

All AI intervention impact parameters are sourced from peer-reviewed clinical guidelines and randomized trial evidence. No impact parameter was assumed without a published evidence base.

Key findings

18.3% reduction in claim rate — from 8.2% to 6.7% of deliveries generating an adverse claim, even at a conservative 69% physician response rate.
Positive ROI at any physician response rate above 20% — sensitivity analysis shows the system still prevents meaningful harm even when most doctors ignore the flags.
Black non-Hispanic patients benefit most — a 23.6% reduction in claim rate for this group, because they carry more untreated guideline gaps the AI can close. The system actively reduces racial disparities.
GBS prophylaxis is the highest-ROI single intervention — $9.4k saved per flag acted on, because neonatal sepsis claims average $210,000 and prophylaxis is near-100% effective.
Monte Carlo 90% CI: −$0.98M to $9.03M saved — even the pessimistic tail shows positive expected value, making this a robust investment case.
